Graphics card prices went the wrong way in 2026. A global memory shortage pushed most current cards well above their launch prices, so “budget GPU” now means something different than it did a year ago. Our Top Picks

🏆 Best Value
AMD Radeon RX 7600 8GB
Launch price: $269

The RX 7600 is the safe choice right now. It is a mature card with stable drivers, it runs current games at 1080p high settings, and it is one of the few current cards that has not climbed far above its launch price this year.

The 8GB of VRAM is its weak spot. It is fine at 1080p today, but you will have to lower texture settings in some new releases. If you keep cards for four or five years, a card with more memory will age better.

+ Pros
  • Close to its launch price, which is rare this year
  • Mature, stable drivers
  • Works well in older PCs too
− Cons
  • Only 8GB of VRAM
  • Ray tracing is weak
  • Not a 1440p card

🥈 Tightest Budget
ASRock Intel Arc B570 Challenger 10GB
Launch price: $219

Intel’s Arc B570 is the cheapest card here that we would still recommend for new games. It has 10GB of VRAM, more than any 8GB card, and in independent tests it comes out ahead of the older RTX 3060 at 1440p while usually costing less. This ASRock Challenger version uses a two-fan cooler.

One thing to check first: Intel Arc cards need Resizable BAR turned on in your motherboard BIOS to perform properly. Most boards from the last few years support it. If your PC is older than that, pick the RX 7600 instead.

+ Pros
  • 10GB of VRAM at the lowest price here
  • Faster than the RTX 3060 in most modern games
  • XeSS upscaling support
− Cons
  • Needs Resizable BAR (older PCs lose a lot of performance)
  • Older DX9/DX11 games can be hit or miss
  • Not built for 1440p at high settings

Be honest about its age. It supports DLSS upscaling but not the newer frame generation, and in raw speed the Arc B570 beats it in most modern games. Buy it only if the price is close to the B570 or you specifically want NVIDIA.

Why GPUs Cost More in 2026

Graphics cards use the same kind of memory chips that are in high demand across the whole tech industry this year. When memory got scarce, card makers raised prices and sales became rare. Hardware press has tracked current-gen cards selling far above launch prices for months.

That changes the advice. Cards that are not inflated much, like the RX 7600, and Intel’s Arc cards now give much better value than the newest NVIDIA and AMD models. If your current card still runs your games, waiting is also a reasonable choice.

💡 Which one should you pick? Most people: RX 7600. Every dollar counts and your PC supports Resizable BAR: Arc B570. You want NVIDIA with 12GB: RTX 3060. You play DLSS games: RTX 5060. You want it to last: RX 9060 XT 16GB.

What to Avoid

  • 8GB versions when a bigger one is close in price: The RX 9060 XT and RTX 5060 Ti both come in 8GB and 16GB versions. If the 16GB card costs only a little more, take it.
  • GTX 10 series (1060, 1070): Too old. Driver support is fading and they cannot run many new games well.
  • Any GPU with less than 8GB VRAM: 4GB and 6GB cards already struggle in newer titles.
  • No-name brands on Amazon: Stick to ASUS, MSI, Sapphire, PowerColor, Gigabyte, ASRock and the other established board makers.
  • Panic buying at peak prices: Prices change week to week this year. Check a couple of listings before you pay.

What to Look for Before You Buy

  • VRAM: 8GB is the minimum for 1080p today. 10 to 12GB is the comfortable middle, and 16GB is what keeps a card useful for years.
  • Resizable BAR: Required for Intel Arc cards to run well. Look for it in your BIOS before you order an Arc card.
  • Power supply: All five cards here are fine with a decent 550W to 650W power supply. Check that your PSU has the right PCIe power connector.
  • Upscaling: DLSS is NVIDIA only. FSR and XeSS work on more hardware. If your favourite games support DLSS 4, that is the strongest reason to pick the RTX 5060.
  • Cooler quality: The chip is the same across brands, but the cooler is not. Models with two or three fans run cooler and quieter than the cheapest single-fan versions.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Pairing a new GPU with a very old CPU: A Ryzen 5 5600 or Core i5-12400 is a sensible minimum for these cards. Older quad-cores will hold them back.
  • Paying extra for RGB: Lighting adds cost, not frames. Pay for the cooler, not the LEDs.
  • Forgetting the monitor: A faster card is wasted on a 60Hz screen. Our best budget gaming monitors under $200 guide covers affordable high refresh rate options.
  • Not cleaning out old drivers: When switching between AMD, NVIDIA and Intel, remove the old driver with DDU first.

Pro Tips for Getting the Most Out of Your GPU

Pro Tip After installing a new GPU, fully uninstall old drivers using DDU (Display Driver Uninstaller) before installing fresh drivers. Old driver remnants cause more performance issues than people realize.
  • Turn on Resizable BAR: It is required for Arc cards and gives a small free boost on AMD and NVIDIA cards in many games.
  • Use upscaling on the Quality preset: DLSS, FSR and XeSS in Quality mode look close to native and add a good amount of FPS.
  • Watch your temps: A new GPU should stay under about 85°C under load. Our guide to monitoring PC temperature while gaming shows how to check.
  • Keep Windows lean: Take time to optimize Windows 11 for gaming and you get more out of your card at no extra cost.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is 8GB VRAM enough for gaming in 2026?

For 1080p at high settings, mostly yes, but you will need to lower textures in some new releases. If you keep cards for several years, 10GB or more is the safer choice, which is why the Arc B570, RTX 3060 12GB and RX 9060 XT 16GB are on this list.

Should I wait for prices to drop?

If your current card still runs what you play, waiting is reasonable. Prices are high because of the memory shortage, and nobody knows exactly when that eases. If you need a card now, the RX 7600 and the Arc B570 are the picks that are not inflated much.

Is the RTX 3060 still worth it in 2026?

Only in specific cases. Its 12GB of VRAM is still useful, but it is a 2021 card and the Arc B570 is usually faster for less. Pick the 3060 if you want an NVIDIA card with 12GB and you find it at a price close to the B570.

Is Intel Arc reliable now?

Intel’s drivers have improved a lot, and the B570 performs well in modern games. The two caveats are the Resizable BAR requirement and occasional problems in older DX9 and DX11 titles. If you mostly play recent games on a recent PC, it is a good choice.

The Verdict

For most budget builders in October 2026, the RX 7600 is the safest buy: proven, stable and close to its launch price. Pick the Arc B570 when money is tightest and your PC supports Resizable BAR, the RTX 3060 12GB if you want NVIDIA with more memory, the RTX 5060 if DLSS 4 matters to you, and the RX 9060 XT 16GB if you can spend more and want a card that lasts.
