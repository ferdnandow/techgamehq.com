What Actually Matters for Mobile Gaming

  • The chip: This decides how heavy games run. Former flagship chips like the Snapdragon 8 Gen 3 and 8 Elite are far ahead of budget chips.
  • Refresh rate: A 120Hz screen makes fast games feel much smoother than 60Hz.
  • Battery: Gaming drains batteries fast. Look for 5,000mAh or more.
  • Cooling: Phones slow down when they get hot. A clip-on phone cooler helps during long sessions.
  • Where it is sold: Buy a model sold officially in the US. Imported phones can miss bands your carrier uses and usually have no US warranty.
💡 What about POCO and Redmi? Phones like the POCO X7 Pro give great gaming performance per dollar, and you will see them in many lists. They are not sold officially in the US, though. That usually means no US warranty and possible problems with some carriers, so we left them out.

Common Mistakes When Buying a Budget Gaming Phone

  • Looking only at RAM: 8GB is enough. The chip matters much more than extra RAM.
  • Ignoring storage: Big games take a lot of space. Get at least 128GB, and 256GB if you install several large games.
  • Buying the cheapest phone for heavy games: A $200 phone will not run Genshin Impact well. Match the phone to the games you actually play.
  • Forgetting accessories: A controller or a cooler can make a bigger difference than a small phone upgrade. Our mobile gaming tips guide covers what helps.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can budget phones run Genshin Impact well?

The OnePlus 13R runs it smoothly at high settings. The Galaxy S24 FE also handles it well, and the Galaxy A37 runs it at lower settings. The Moto G Power is not a good choice for it.

Is 8GB RAM enough for gaming?

Yes. For gaming, the chip and cooling matter much more than going from 8GB to 12GB of RAM.

Should I wait for a newer model?

Phones from the previous generation often get the biggest discounts, and a former flagship chip usually beats a brand-new budget chip in games. If one of these is on sale, it is a good time to buy.
